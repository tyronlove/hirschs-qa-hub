Structure recommendation

The brief asked whether every question needs its own page, or whether passage indexing can surface answers in place. The answer is neither extreme — it's a two-tier split, and the deciding factor is whether the question has a product attached to it.

On passage indexing specifically: it lets Google rank a single long page for a narrow sub-question buried mid-page, even when that question isn't in the title or H1. That's a strong argument for consolidating low-value questions — it removes the old excuse of “we need a separate page or it'll never rank.” What it does not do is remove the reasons a high-value question still wants its own page: a unique URL to link to and earn links with, per-question performance data, and a product shelf that can only be matched one-question-at-a-time. Passage indexing is a reason to build fewer, better pages — not zero pages.

The failure mode to avoid is the one in the original Cars.co.za structure: sending a user to a new page to read two sentences. That's a wasted click and a thin page, and it's now unnecessary.
